=====================================================
Semtech GN25L95 PON laser driver: the RTL9602C optics
=====================================================

Overview
========

The transmitter and the receiver of the stick are driven by a laser-driver IC
that sits between the SoC and the fibre, taken to be a Semtech GN25L95. The SoC
reaches it over its I2C master 1. The IC serves the SFF-8472 management
interface of an SFP module, the page A0h at the 7-bit address ``0x50`` and the
page A2h at ``0x51``, and it keeps its configuration and calibration in an
EEPROM of its own. Two more connections run from the SoC to the optics: the
hard transmit-disable input, TX_DISABLE, taken to be SoC GPIO 13, and the burst
enable, which the GPON MAC keys for each upstream burst.

The IC has a driver of its own, ``gn25l95`` (``drivers/net/pon/gn25l95.c``), an
I2C client driver bound by the IC's device tree node. It does five things:

- It moves bytes over the I2C bus, one per transfer or the two of a diagnostic
  word together, through the SMBus adapter ``rtl9602c i2c`` that the master's
  own driver, ``i2c-rtl9602c``, registers.
- It publishes the five measurements of the SFF-8472 diagnostics, which are the
  temperature, the supply voltage, the transmitter bias, the transmitted power
  and the received power, as the hwmon device ``gn25l95``.
- It offers the optics to the GPON MAC through the PON class
  (Documentation/networking/pon.rst): the MAC asserts TX_DISABLE below O3 and
  releases it from O3 on, prepares the transmitter before every activation, and
  asks for the transmit power levels that the OLT sets and that the ONU takes
  itself before it is ranged.
- Before every activation it puts back the configuration the laser driver
  loads from its EEPROM at power-up: the automatic extinction-ratio loop on,
  and the power control's set point of transmit power level mode 0. This is
  the factory configuration.
- It offers two debugfs views that only read, ``a2`` and ``eeblocks``, a third,
  ``tables``, that selects each table of the upper page to read it, and, with
  ``CONFIG_PON_GN25L95_STORE``, an entry, ``t2write``, that stores a byte of the
  configuration table in the module's EEPROM, or resets the laser driver, when
  root writes to it.

The driver writes nothing to the laser driver at probe. Its only writes are
those of the factory configuration, at PON bring-up, those of the transmit
power levels, those a read of the ``tables`` view makes and those a write to
``t2write`` asks for. Whether they reach the part is the bus's to decide: the
switch driver, ``rtl9602c``, lets the adapter pass writes on only for a module
it has identified as the one this page was measured on, and never with
``CONFIG_RTL9602C_OPTICS_RO`` (see "The modules the driver writes" and "The
read-only build"). The connections::

    RTL9602C                               laser-driver IC
    +---------------+                      +------------------------+
    | I2C master 1  |==== SCL, SDA ========| A0h (0x50), A2h (0x51) |
    |               |                      | configuration EEPROM   |
    | GPIO 13       |--- TX_DISABLE ------>|                        |
    |               |                      |   drives the           |
    | GPON MAC      |--- burst enable ---->|   transmitter and      |
    |               |                      |   the receiver         |
    +---------------+                      +------------------------+

The GPON MAC is described in
Documentation/networking/device_drivers/pon/rtl9602c-gpon.rst and the PON MAC,
together with the overview of the set, in
Documentation/networking/device_drivers/pon/rtl9602c.rst. The switch is
described in Documentation/networking/dsa/rtl9602c.rst, the PON class in
Documentation/networking/pon.rst, and the GPIO controller and the pad control in
Documentation/arch/mips/rtl9602c.rst.

neither ``CONFIG_I2C_CHARDEV`` nor ``CONFIG_RTL9602C_OPTICS_RO``.

Device tree and binding
-----------------------

The laser driver is a child of the ``i2c`` node of the ``switch`` node
(compatible ``realtek,rtl9602c-switch``), which describes the SoC's I2C master
1. ``arch/mips/boot/dts/realtek/rtl9602c.dtsi`` gives the ``i2c`` node, and the
board's tree, ``dfp-34x-2c3.dts``, the device on it and the phandle by which
the GPON MAC finds it::

    &switch {
            ...
            realtek,pon-optics = <&laser>;
    };

    &switch_i2c {
            laser: laser-driver@50 {
                    compatible = "semtech,gn25l95";
                    reg = <0x50>;
                    tx-disable-gpios = <&gpio0 13 GPIO_ACTIVE_HIGH>;
            };
    };

The bindings are
``Documentation/devicetree/bindings/net/semtech,gn25l95.yaml``, for the laser
driver, and
``Documentation/devicetree/bindings/net/dsa/realtek,rtl9602c-switch.yaml``, for
the ``i2c`` node and ``realtek,pon-optics``. ``reg`` is the address of A0h;
A2h is at the next address, which the driver takes with a dummy client so
that no other client takes it. ``tx-disable-gpios`` is the hard
transmit-disable input, with one entry and not required: while the input is
asserted the transmitter emits no light. ``gn25l95_probe()`` takes the line
with ``devm_gpiod_get_optional()`` under the name ``tx-disable``, already
driven to its asserted level (``GPIOD_OUT_HIGH``), so that nothing lases before
the PON path allows it. Without the property every TX_DISABLE step is skipped.
GPIO 13, active high, is the laser driver's disable input: driving it high
stops the light (measured, see "TX_DISABLE").

The GPIO controller, ``gpio@18003300`` (compatible ``realtek,rtl9602c-gpio``
and ``realtek,otto-gpio``), and the pad-function registers are described in
Documentation/arch/mips/rtl9602c.rst.

Interfaces to user space
------------------------

- The hwmon device ``gn25l95``, with the five measurements of the diagnostics.
  Its directory is ``/sys/class/hwmon/hwmonN``, where N follows the order of
  registration, and it is found by its ``name`` file::

      # grep -l gn25l95 /sys/class/hwmon/hwmon*/name
      /sys/class/hwmon/hwmon0/name

  The attributes are ``temp1_input``, ``in0_input``, ``curr1_input``,
  ``power1_input`` and ``power2_input``, each with a ``_label`` file and the
  four thresholds ``_lcrit``, ``_min``, ``_max`` and ``_crit``, all read-only
  (mode ``0444``). Their units are those of the hwmon interface: millidegree
  Celsius, millivolt, milliampere and microwatt (see "Diagnostics"). Reading
  a value or a threshold takes one two-byte transfer on the I2C master;
  reading a label takes none.
- The I2C adapter ``rtl9602c i2c``, whose name is in
  ``/sys/class/i2c-adapter/i2c-N/name``, and on it the device ``N-0050``, bound
  to ``gn25l95``, and the dummy ``N-0051``. The adapter offers SMBus byte-data
  transfers and word-data reads (Documentation/i2c/busses/i2c-rtl9602c.rst).
  With ``CONFIG_I2C_CHARDEV`` it has a file ``/dev/i2c-N``; the two addresses
  are taken, so a client of it reaches them only with ``I2C_SLAVE_FORCE``, and
  a write the adapter passes on can change the module for good (see "The
  management interface").
- ``a2``, ``eeblocks`` and ``tables`` in the client's debugfs directory,
  ``/sys/kernel/debug/i2c/i2c-N/N-0050/``, views that read the laser driver one
  byte at a time and print it (see "The debugfs views"). They have mode
  ``0400`` and exist only with ``CONFIG_DEBUG_FS``. The view of the SoC's SFP
  slave, ``/sys/kernel/debug/rtl9602c/sfpslave``, is the switch driver's.
- ``t2write`` in the same directory, mode ``0200``, which stores a byte of
  table 2 in the module's EEPROM or resets the laser driver (see "Storing
  table 2 bytes"), only with ``CONFIG_PON_GN25L95_STORE``.
- The TX_DISABLE line. It has no file of its own: it is line 13 of the GPIO
  bank, requested under the consumer name ``tx-disable``, and
  ``/sys/kernel/debug/gpio`` shows its direction and level (high while
  TX_DISABLE is asserted). Its level follows the ONU state, so
  ``ip link set pon up`` and ``ip link set pon down`` are the commands that
  move it (see "TX_DISABLE").
- ``ip link set pon up`` also puts the factory configuration back, whose
  writes the adapter refuses with ``CONFIG_RTL9602C_OPTICS_RO`` or for a module
  the switch driver does not know (see "The factory configuration" and "The
  modules the driver writes").
- The kernel log lines of the driver, which begin with ``gn25l95 N-0050:``, and
  those of the switch's side, which begin with ``rtl9602c-switch:`` (see
  "Logging").
- The driver has no module parameters, devlink parameters or ethtool
  statistics.

First checks on a board
-----------------------

1. Read ``eeblocks`` in the client's debugfs directory. The blocks for ``0x50``
   and ``0x51`` report no unanswered read, and A0h byte 0 reads ``03``, the SFP
   identifier. The blocks for ``0x52`` and ``0x53`` read ``ff`` throughout, with
   at most one unanswered read in 256 (see "The debugfs views").
2. Look for the hwmon device. If it is missing, either ``CONFIG_HWMON`` is off
   or the kernel log carries ``gn25l95 N-0050: no diagnostics`` (see
   "Logging"), and the ``eeblocks`` view shows whether anything answers.
3. Read the five measurements (see "The hwmon device"). Below O3, with
   TX_DISABLE asserted, the bias and the transmitted power read 0; in O5 they
   read the burst values. The received power shows whether light from the OLT
   reaches the receiver (see "Loss of signal and received power").

How to read the fact tables
===========================

Each block below has a short account of what the hardware does and what the
driver does with it, followed by fact tables with three columns: *Fact*, *Tag*
and *Checked by*. The tag says how the fact is established. **M**, measured on
the board (the DFP-34X-2C3): *Checked by* gives the command or observation,
through the driver's own interfaces (the kernel log, hwmon, debugfs), that shows
it. **T**, tested by function: the driver depends on the fact, and the named
behaviour fails if it is wrong. **P**, public standard: *Checked by* names the
document and the clause. **I**, inferred, not verified: the fact is an
assumption, and *Checked by* says what would confirm or refute it. The last
chapter collects these facts and says which of them the driver depends on.

A row with two tags is established in two ways, or in two parts; *Checked by*
says which part carries which tag. Register offsets are from the base of the
switch register window, physical ``0x1b000000``. The driver reaches the I2C
registers with 32-bit accesses, and a byte travels in the low 8 bits. Bytes of
the laser driver are named by their SFF-8472 page and number: A0h byte 40 is
byte 40 of the page at the 7-bit address ``0x50``, and A2h byte 96 is byte 96 of
the page at ``0x51``. The behaviours that a tested fact depends on are named in
words: the optics diagnostics read, and the ONU reaches O5 on an OLT.

The I2C master
==============

The SoC has two I2C masters with the same set of registers, and the laser
driver answers on master 1 only. The master has a driver of its own,
``i2c-rtl9602c`` (Documentation/i2c/busses/i2c-rtl9602c.rst), which owns its
registers, the pad function that enables it and the order of a transfer, and
registers the SMBus adapter ``rtl9602c i2c`` with the children of the switch
node's ``i2c`` node, the laser driver among them. The switch driver makes the
auxiliary device that driver binds to, with the switch's register window
(``rtl9602c_optics_i2c_add()``), and is told when the adapter appears
(``rtl9602c_optics_adapter()``): that identifies the module, which decides
whether the adapter passes writes on. The driver of this page moves its bytes
through the I2C core, with ``__i2c_smbus_xfer()``, in sequences under the locks
below.

The management interface
========================

The laser driver serves the two pages of the management interface of an SFP
module: A0h, the serial ID, at the 7-bit address ``0x50``, and A2h, the
diagnostics and the module's own controls, at ``0x51``. The part runs from the
configuration and calibration in its own EEPROM, and the driver writes nothing
to it at probe. The addresses ``0x52`` and ``0x53`` acknowledge as well, but
every byte reads ``0xff`` there, so the stored configuration cannot be read
through them.

The driver numbers the registers of the part from ``0x000`` to ``0x1ff``, the
way ``gn25l95_read()`` takes them:

.. list-table::
   :header-rows: 1
   :widths: 20 20 60

   * - Number
     - Device
     - Byte
   * - ``0x000`` to ``0x0ff``
     - ``0x50``
     - A0h byte n.
   * - ``0x100`` to ``0x1ff``
     - ``0x51``
     - A2h byte n - ``0x100`` (``GN25L95_A2`` = ``0x100``).
   * - ``0x200`` and above
     - none
     - Refused with ``-EINVAL``.

In the rest of this page a byte is named by its page and its byte number, as
SFF-8472 does. The driver names three places in A2h: ``GN25L95_A2_DDM``
(``0x60``, the first diagnostic word), ``GN25L95_A2_PASSWORD`` (``0x7b``, byte
123, the first of four bytes inside the range 120-126 that SFF-8472 leaves to
the module maker) and ``GN25L95_A2_PAGE`` (``0x7f``, the page select, byte
127).

Diagnostics
===========

A2h bytes 96 to 105 hold five 16-bit measurements, most significant byte first.
The module reports them in the units SFF-8472 gives an internally calibrated
module, and the driver applies no calibration. The driver reads each value in
one transfer of two bytes, the most significant byte first, during which the
module does not update it, as SFF-8472 has a measurement read. The hwmon device
converts each value to the unit of the hwmon interface for its type
(``Documentation/ABI/testing/sysfs-class-hwmon``), as ``drivers/net/phy/sfp.c``
does, rounded to the nearest; the ``a2`` view prints the values in the SFF-8472
units.

The debugfs views
-----------------

The ``a2`` and ``eeblocks`` views read and never write what they show. They
are created in the client's debugfs directory,
``/sys/kernel/debug/i2c/i2c-N/N-0050/``, with mode ``0400``, N being the
adapter's number. The ``sfpslave`` view, of the switch driver, is in
``/sys/kernel/debug/rtl9602c/`` and only reads too::

    # cat /sys/kernel/debug/i2c/i2c-0/0-0050/a2
    # cat /sys/kernel/debug/i2c/i2c-0/0-0050/eeblocks
    # cat /sys/kernel/debug/rtl9602c/sfpslave

The ``a2`` view reads A2h bytes 0 to 127 one byte at a time, stopping at the
first failed read with the line ``A2h read failed at 0x<byte>: <error>``, and
prints them as eight rows of sixteen bytes. It then prints the five diagnostic
words in the SFF-8472 units, bytes 123 to 126 and byte 127::

    00: <16 bytes>
    ...
    70: <16 bytes>

    temperature <t> C
    vcc         <v> V
    tx bias     <b> uA
    tx power    <p> uW
    rx power    <r> uW   <- light on the fibre

    password 0x7b-0x7e  <byte 123> <byte 124> <byte 125> <byte 126>
    table select 0x7f = 0x<byte 127>

The ``eeblocks`` view reads all 256 bytes of each of the devices ``0x50`` to
``0x53`` and prints each as a block of sixteen rows, headed by the device, the
place of the block in a 1 KiB image and the number of reads that went
unanswered::

    === i2c 0x50 -- image 0x000 -- <n> of 256 did not answer
    00: <16 bytes>
    ...
    f0: <16 bytes>

The blocks follow for ``0x51`` (image ``0x100``), ``0x52`` (``0x200``) and
``0x53`` (``0x300``). A block in which every read went unanswered is replaced by
the word ``silent``. An unanswered read shows as ``00`` in the dump. The view
never writes byte 127, so A2h bytes 128 to 255 show the page that byte selects
at that moment, which is page ``02h`` while a bring-up or a transmit power
level runs.

The ``sfpslave`` view reads the SoC's SFP slave: an I2C slave that can serve
a 512-byte RAM to the host as a module's identity, A0h then A2h. It prints
the slave's two words, ``aux`` and ``ctl`` (``RTL9602C_SFPSLV_AUX`` and
``RTL9602C_SFPSLV_CTL``), whether the slave is enabled and how, then the RAM,
read a byte at a time through the access register
(``RTL9602C_SFPSLV_RAM_ACC``), as two blocks headed ``A0h`` and ``A2h``. A byte
whose read does not complete ends the dump with a line saying so. The view
writes only the access register's command, to read.

What the host in the cage reads is not the SoC's slave on this board. Measured
on one stick, with this driver, from both sides at once (2026-10-17): the slave
is disabled (``RTL9602C_SFPSLV_CTL_ENABLE`` 0) and its RAM holds 512 zero
bytes, while the host reads at ``0x50`` an A0h byte for byte equal to the laser
driver's and at ``0x51`` a static page left over from another module's EEPROM,
not the laser driver's diagnostics, which the SoC reads at its own ``0x51`` at
the same moment. The host's pair is therefore a separate memory on a bus the
SoC does not reach: the stick cannot change what the host reads, and a host
sees no diagnostics and no loss-of-signal state through it. Why the two A0h
pages are equal the measurement does not say.

The cage's RX_LOS contact is the laser driver's own loss-of-signal output,
not the SoC's. Measured on the same stick (2026-10-17), the host reading the
line through its GPIO driver: with the fibre pulled twice, for 9 s and 42 s,
the line was high within a second of the PON leaving O5 and low again within a
second of light returning, and low at every other moment. The SoC's LED output
15, which the shipped firmware drives on a loss of signal,
held on, off and inverted in CPU force mode with the PON kind, left the line
low. So the host has a loss-of-signal signal with no help from the SoC, and
this driver drives none. A host that honours byte 65 of the A0h page in front
of it takes the link down while the line is high; that page, written on the
host's side, declares no RX_LOS on this stick, which keeps the stick reachable
over the link while the fibre is dark.

The ``tables`` view reads A2h bytes 128 to 255 for each table the page select
can show, 0, 1, 2, 4, 5 and 6, and prints each as eight rows of sixteen bytes
under a line ``=== table <n>``. It puts byte 127 back as it found it at the end, also when a table fails (``=== table <n>:
failed: <error>``). Table 2 holds the laser driver's configuration, among it
the receiver's loss-of-signal control (BBh) and the APD bias control (BDh);
table 6 the temperature-indexed APD DAC values at C0h to FFh. Byte 127 is kept
in the module's EEPROM, so a read of the view writes the EEPROM once for each
table it shows. On a bus that refuses writes, as with
``CONFIG_RTL9602C_OPTICS_RO``, the view shows the table selected and the others
fail with ``-EPERM``::

    # cat /sys/kernel/debug/i2c/i2c-0/0-0050/tables
    === table 0
    80: <16 bytes>
    ...

Storing table 2 bytes
---------------------

The configuration table, table 2, is the laser driver's configuration as its
maker sets it: among it the receiver's loss-of-signal control (BBh), whose
``LOS_POLARITY`` bit decides how the receiver's signal detect has to be read
(``rtl9602c-gpon.rst``, "Downstream lock, LOS and alarms"), the APD bias
control (BDh) and the current limits (ADh-AFh). A module whose table 2 has gone
wrong is put right through ``t2write``, mode ``0200``, in a kernel built with
``CONFIG_PON_GN25L95_STORE``, one command per write::

    # W=/sys/kernel/debug/i2c/i2c-0/0-0050/t2write
    # echo open > $W
    # echo "bb 1c" > $W
    # echo close > $W
    # echo reset > $W
    # cat /sys/kernel/debug/i2c/i2c-0/0-0050/tables

``open`` writes ``0xff`` to the password bytes 123 to 126, shows table 2 and
clears both bits of EEPROM_CTRL (C7h), so that the part copies the changes of
its registers into its EEPROM; it keeps C7h and the page select as found.
``REG VALUE``, both hexadecimal, writes the password bytes and shows table 2
again, so that a page switched in between cannot send the byte to another
table, then writes ``VALUE`` at table 2 byte ``REG``. The byte is not read
back: while the part copies into its EEPROM it does not acknowledge and its
reads return a constant, so a read then proves nothing. ``close`` puts C7h and
the page select back as found. The kernel log has a line for each step,
beginning ``gn25l95 N-0050: table 2``.

Only the bytes that a repair of a module has needed are taken, the others fail
with ``EINVAL``, as does a byte before ``open``: the current limits ADh-AFh,
B6h-B8h, the receiver's loss-of-signal control BBh, its APD bias control BDh
and the ADC control C1h. Not the other bytes of B1h-BDh, among which are the
transmitter's fault, alarm and rogue ONU controls; which of B1h-BAh holds which
control is not recorded, so that B6h-B8h hold none of the rogue ONU controls is
not known. Not SAFE_MODE_STARTUP (A0h), whose wrong value turns the transmitter
off for good; not the calibrated DACs (A8h-ACh) and constants (D2h-E7h), which
belong to the one module; not the sleep control (BFh), the status (C0h), the
part's I2C address (C2h, C3h), the EEPROM controls (C4h-C8h), the passwords or
the monitors.

``reset``, with table 2 closed, restarts the part's state machine with its reset
key, ``5D 2C 6A C9`` at bytes 123 to 126 and ``0B`` at the page select, which
loads the tables from the EEPROM as at power-up; the entry then writes ``0xff``
to the password bytes, as ``open`` does, and puts the page select back. The laser
goes through its start-up again, so the PON drops for that time. A read of the
``tables`` view after ``reset`` shows what the EEPROM holds, which is how a
stored byte is checked without taking the module out.

TX_DISABLE
==========

The laser has a hard disable input, TX_DISABLE: while it is asserted the
transmitter emits no light. The device tree gives the line as SoC GPIO 13,
active high, and asserting it with the ONU in O5 stopped the light (see the
rows below). The GPON state machine drives the
line: asserted in every state below O3, released from O3 on, because an ONU
first transmits in the Serial-Number state, O3, answering a serial number
request. Releasing the line does not make the laser emit continuously: the GPON
MAC still keys each burst. Below O3 the line is the only hardware block on the
light besides the GPON MAC not keying bursts.

   * - O3 serial number, O4 ranging, O5 operation

The factory configuration
=========================

The laser driver loads its configuration table from its EEPROM at power-up and
runs from it, the automatic extinction-ratio loop on among it: the loop sets
the modulation current, and the power control loop moves the bias to hold the
transmitted power at its set point. The shipped firmware is taken to
leave the part so (see the rows below). At every PON bring-up, before the
first O3, this driver puts that configuration back: it turns the loop on and
writes the set point of transmit power level mode 0. It writes both because the
table's registers keep what is written to them across a warm reboot of the
SoC, so a kernel can find the loop off, or a lower set point, left by an
earlier one. The writes are to registers only: the module's persistence
control keeps them out of the EEPROM, and a power cycle gives the module its
own configuration back.

Until 2026-10-21 the driver turned the loop off at every bring-up instead, so
that the modulation ran from the value the module loads at power-up: the
modulation hold. A measurement of 2026-09-22 had found the OLT provisioning
no activation with the loop on at a long burst overhead. That no longer
reproduces: activations with the loop on and with it off came out the same,
down to a burst overhead of 12 bytes (see the rows below).

The set point of mode 0 is the one the first bring-up since probe finds. The
driver cannot tell whether that is the module's own: after a reset that shut
no device down while the ONU sent at a lower level, it is that level's (see
"Transmit power levelling"). The module's own is in the EEPROM, which the SoC
does not read (``0x52`` and ``0x53`` read ``0xff``, see "The management
interface"); only the part's reset loads it into the table again, and that
reset writes bytes the module may keep (see the rows below). So the bring-up
uses plain register writes, and does not reset the part.

128-255 while A2h byte 127 holds ``02h``. The driver calls it table 2. The
registers it uses are listed by their A2h byte numbers (the driver's macros are

the first O3, when the GPON MAC prepares the optics (``gn25l95_start()``). On a
bus that refuses writes, with ``CONFIG_RTL9602C_OPTICS_RO`` or for a module the
switch driver does not know, its first write is refused and it returns 0
having written nothing. Its steps:

1. Read A2h byte 127 and keep the value; write ``02h`` to it unless it holds
   ``02h`` already. Purpose: show the configuration table at A2h 128-255, and
   remember the page to restore. (T)
2. Read PERSIST, ER_CTRL and the set point. Purpose: the values the next steps
   decide on and log. (T)
3. If bits 1:0 of PERSIST are not both set, log ``EEPROM updates are enabled``
   and give up with ``-EPERM``. Purpose: a table write now would be copied into
   the EEPROM, so none is made. (M) By then step 1 has written the page select,
   when it held another page, which the module keeps.
4. Take the set point of level mode 0: the one kept since the first bring-up
   since probe, or, at that first bring-up, the one read in step 2. Purpose:
   the level the module came with, as far as its registers tell. (M, I)
5. Write that set point, then ER_CTRL with bit 0 set, and read both back.
   Purpose: the power at mode 0, which undoes a lower level left by an earlier
   session, and the loop on, which undoes a loop turned off by an earlier
   kernel. Both are written at every bring-up, so that a bus that refuses
   writes shows here. (M)
6. If bit 0 does not read back set, or the set point reads back otherwise, log
   ``the factory configuration did not take`` and give up with ``-EIO``.
   Purpose: say that the module may not run as it loads itself. (I)
7. Keep the set point as that of mode 0, count the optics at mode 0, and log
   ER_CTRL and the set point as found and as left. Purpose: the lower levels
   are made from that set point, and mode 0 puts it back. (T)
8. Write the value kept in step 1 back to byte 127. Purpose: leave the page
   selection as it was found. This follows every step after the write of
   step 1, whatever its outcome; only a transfer that fails in the read of
   step 1 returns before it, when the page select has not been written. (T)

A failed bring-up of the optics is logged by the GPON bring-up (``the optics
did not prepare the transmitter``), and the bring-up carries on.

Transmit power levelling
========================

ITU-T G.984.2 gives the ONU's transmitter three power levels, the level modes:
0, normal, and 1 and 2, 3 dB and 6 dB below it. The ONU starts each activation
at the mode the OLT's Upstream_Overhead names, takes the next mode itself after
unanswered serial-number requests, and the OLT moves it between them with
Change_Power_Level (see "Transmit power level" in
Documentation/networking/device_drivers/pon/rtl9602c-gpon.rst). The driver
makes level mode 0 the power the module came with and sets the lower modes
from it, in the configuration table of "The factory configuration", shown and
kept out of the EEPROM in the same way.

The part's power control loop moves the bias until the monitor photodiode
reaches a set point, so the transmitted power follows the set point while the
bias has room, and the automatic extinction-ratio loop sets the modulation for
the bias it finds. So a lower level is set by the set point alone. Whether the
loops reach 6 dB down before the bias reaches its floor is not measured. With
the loop off and the modulation held, as until 2026-10-21, the bias reached
its floor about 1.5 dB down, and the driver lowered the modulation by the same
factor as the power before it lowered the set point; the measurements of that
driver are kept in the rows below.

The set point of a lower level is found by measuring, and the module measures
the power only in bursts, which come often only from O4 on. So the MAC says
with each request whether the power can be measured: in O4 and O5 the driver
finds the set point and keeps it for the mode; before, where the ONU sends only
its answers to serial-number requests, it writes the set point kept for the
mode without measuring, and leaves a mode it has not measured since probe
unset, with the optics where they are.

The modules the driver writes
=============================

Who may write the part is the switch driver's to decide, which knows the board,
not this driver's. When the adapter comes, ``rtl9602c_optics_identify()`` of
the switch driver reads the vendor name (A0h bytes 20-35) and the vendor part
number (A0h bytes 40-55) the module declares, the header the host reads through
the SFP cage, and drops the trailing spaces. The adapter passes writes on only
when the name is ``ODI`` and the part number begins with ``DFP-34X-2C``, the
module this page was measured on. For any other module, and while the header
cannot be read, the adapter refuses every write with ``-EPERM``, as in the
read-only build below, and this driver only reads the part: the bring-up
returns 0 having written nothing and logs the refusal once, no transmit power
level is set, and the switch driver logs one line (see "Logging"). A header
that cannot be read is read again every five seconds until it reads, so that a
transfer that failed once, when the adapter came, does not leave the module
unwritten until the next boot.

The read-only build
===================

``CONFIG_RTL9602C_OPTICS_RO``, an option of the switch driver, makes the I2C
adapter refuse every write with ``-EPERM``, from this driver and from any other
client, user space through ``i2c-dev`` included. Nothing on the board then
changes the module: the page select, the bytes 123 to 126 and the configuration
table stay as the module has them. After a module power-up that is the factory
configuration; after a warm reboot from a kernel that wrote the table, a loop
it turned off or a lower set point it left stays so until the module loses
power. The bring-up returns 0 having written nothing and logs the refusal once.
No
transmit power level is set: a request for one gives ``-EOPNOTSUPP``, and the
ONU stays at level mode 0. The hwmon device and the ``a2`` and ``eeblocks``
views only read, and they are unchanged; the ``tables`` view shows the table
selected.

Setup, in order
===============

The steps are in the order they are taken. The tag in parentheses at the end
of a step is the tag of the fact the step rests on (see the tables above).

1. In switch setup, make the auxiliary device ``rtl9602c.i2c``, for the
   master's driver to set ``RTL9602C_PADMUX_FUNC_EN_I2C`` in
   ``RTL9602C_PADMUX_FUNC_EN`` by read-modify-write and register the master as
   the adapter ``rtl9602c i2c``, with the children of the ``i2c`` node.
   Purpose: without the enable the master ignores every command. (M)
2. The I2C core registers the laser driver's device, ``N-0050``, and binds
   this driver: request the ``tx-disable`` line as an output driven to its
   asserted level, take ``0x51`` with a dummy client, read the temperature word
   once (A2h bytes 96 and 97) and, if it reads, register the hwmon device
   ``gn25l95``, create the debugfs views, and last offer the optics to the PON
   class. Purpose: nothing lases before the PON path allows it; no hwmon device
   appears when nothing answers at A2h; the MAC finds the optics only once they
   work. (I for the line, M for the rest)
3. When the adapter is there, the switch driver reads the module's vendor name
   and part number, and with them whether the adapter passes writes on (see
   "The modules the driver writes"), and then gives the TX_DISABLE pad to its
   GPIO function (see "TX_DISABLE"). Purpose: a module the code was not
   measured on is only read. (I)
4. The GPON MAC's driver binds once the optics are offered: it takes them by
   ``realtek,pon-optics``, linked to them so that the driver core unbinds the
   MAC before the optics go. Purpose: no MAC without the means to keep the
   laser dark. (I)
5. At the first ONU state change after ``ip link set pon up``, which is O1, and
   at every later one, the MAC drives TX_DISABLE (see "TX_DISABLE"). Purpose:
   follow the activation state. (T)
6. In the GPON bring-up, after the MAC reset and before the first O3, the MAC
   prepares the optics: the factory configuration (see "The factory
   configuration"). Purpose: the module runs as it loads itself at power-up,
   whatever an earlier kernel left in its registers, at level mode 0. (M)

At shutdown, restart or halt the driver puts level mode 0 back
(``gn25l95_shutdown()``).

The hwmon device, the dummy client and the optics are device-managed and go
with the laser driver's device, whose debugfs directory the I2C core removes
with it. The adapter goes with the master's auxiliary device, which goes with
the switch's platform device.

Known limitations
=================

- Without ``CONFIG_RTL9602C_OPTICS_RO``, for a module the switch driver knows,
  the I2C adapter passes every write on, and A0h and A2h bytes 0-127 take
  writes without a password and keep them across a power cycle of the module,
  so a client of the adapter can change the module for good. User space
  reaches the adapter through ``i2c-dev`` where ``CONFIG_I2C_CHARDEV`` is set,
  and the addresses this driver holds with ``I2C_SLAVE_FORCE``.
- A watchdog reset, or any reset that shuts no device down, with the
  transmit power at level mode 1 or 2 leaves the module there, and the next
  kernel takes that level's set point for the module's own until the module
  loses power (see "Transmit power levelling"): a level asked for then is
  counted down from the lower one. The module's own set point is in its
  EEPROM, which only the part's reset loads into the table again, and the
  bring-up does not use the reset, which writes bytes the module may keep
  (see "The factory configuration"). The power at mode 0 then reads below the
  module's own, about 1690 uW on this board, in hwmon ``power1_input``; a
  module power cycle ends it. The ONU sends at a lower level only from O3 to
  O6, at the mode the OLT names or asks for or, in O3, at the one it took
  itself after unanswered serial-number requests; below O3 and in O7 the GPON
  MAC asks for mode 0. The extinction ratio at the lower levels is not
  measured. A loop turned off by an earlier kernel is turned on again by the
  bring-up.
- The transmit power levels were measured only with the automatic loop off.
  With the loop on, as the driver now runs the part, whether the set point
  alone reaches modes 1 and 2 is not measured (see "Transmit power
  levelling"); a level the search cannot reach gives ``-ERANGE``, and the
  ONU stays at mode 0 and says so.
- Before the ONU is ranged a lower level is set only at a set point kept from
  a measurement since probe, and the module measures a level only when the
  OLT asks for it with Change_Power_Level in O4 or O5. Until it has, the
  default mode of Upstream_Overhead and the ONU's own steps leave the optics
  at mode 0, and Serial_Number_ONU says so. A kept set point is not measured
  again until Change_Power_Level asks for its mode.
- A PON bring-up writes a byte that the module keeps only when A2h byte 127
  holds another page than ``02h``: before it has read PERSIST, the bring-up
  writes ``02h`` there, and it puts the page back at the end. At rest the
  byte holds ``02h`` on this board, so a bring-up writes nothing the module
  keeps.

Open questions
==============

These are the facts tagged **I** in the tables above, each with what would
settle it. The first list holds the assumptions the driver depends on: the
driver is correct on this board only if they hold. The second list holds the
questions to which the code is indifferent: it works whichever way they
resolve.

Assumptions the driver depends on
---------------------------------

.. list-table::
   :header-rows: 1
   :widths: 58 42

   * - Assumption
     - What would settle it
   * - The level this driver requests at probe reaches the pin when the switch
       driver sets bit 13 of ``RTL9602C_PADMUX_GPIO_EN``, after the master's
       device is made: that holds when this driver has bound by then, as with
       both drivers built in, when the pad is a GPIO function at reset, or when
       the bit is not needed ("TX_DISABLE").
     - A run with the bit clear shows whether it is needed at all.
   * - Nothing serialises a transfer between its users ("Known limitations").
     - Reading the hwmon attributes in a loop while reading the ``a2`` view: it
       would show mixed or misplaced bytes.
   * - Two one-byte reads of a diagnostic word can mix two samples ("Known
       limitations").
     - Reading the received-power word in a tight loop while the level changes,
       looking for jumps of about 256 counts.
   * - That the extinction ratio stays as it is at transmit power level modes
       1 and 2, the automatic loop holding it while the bias falls ("Transmit
       power levelling").
     - The eye of the transmitted light at each mode, with an optical
       instrument.
   * - That with the automatic loop on the set point alone takes the power
       3 dB and 6 dB down before the bias reaches its floor, that the power
       read 20 ms after a step is the settled one, and that a kept set point
       gives its mode's power again without measuring ("Transmit power
       levelling").
     - Change_Power_Level injected in O5, down twice and up twice, then
       Upstream_Overhead naming modes 1 and 2 in O2: the logged power and
       hwmon ``power1_input`` and ``curr1_input`` at each mode.
   * - That the set point the first bring-up since probe finds is the module's
       own ("The factory configuration").
     - The bring-up's first log line after a module power cycle against the
       one after a warm reboot; the ``tables`` view after ``reset`` of
       ``t2write`` in a test build.
   * - The read-only build still exposes a writable adapter ("The read-only
       build").
     - Checking the image's configuration for ``CONFIG_I2C_CHARDEV`` settles
       whether user space can reach the adapter, without writing to the part.
