==========================================
Realtek RTL9602C PON MAC and packet buffer
==========================================

Overview
========

The RTL9602C is a SoC for GPON optical network units (ONUs). Three blocks on
the PON side of its switch turn the switch's PON port, port 2, into a link over
a fibre to an optical line terminal (OLT):

- The GPON MAC is the transmission convergence layer of ITU-T G.984.3. It
  receives the downstream frames of the OLT with their PLOAM messages and
  bandwidth maps, runs the ONU's activation from O1 to O5, builds the upstream
  bursts in the allocations the OLT grants, and holds the tables that say which
  Alloc-IDs and GEM Port-IDs belong to the ONU.
- The PON MAC sits between the GPON MAC and the switch's PON port. It is a
  packet buffer for each direction, which keeps frames in on-chip SRAM and
  spills them into DRAM, and, upstream, the streams, the queues and the T-CONT
  scheduler that decide when a frame is offered to the GPON MAC. When the
  driver builds it, it also makes a few settings in the switch that the OMCI
  path needs. This page is about the PON MAC.
- The optics are the laser driver on the stick and the transmitter and
  receiver it runs. The GPON MAC drives them through the PON SerDes and an
  analog front end, and the SoC reaches the laser driver's management
  interface over an I2C master.

The GPON MAC and the PON MAC sit in the register window of the switch, physical
``0x1b000000``, together with the I2C masters that reach the optics. One
driver, ``rtl9602c``, drives all of them and the switch itself. The driver is a
DSA driver, and it offers the PON side through the PON class
(Documentation/networking/pon.rst): an OMCI control port, a network interface
for each GEM port, and a generic netlink family. This page gives the overview
of the three blocks and describes the PON MAC, the GEM ports as network
interfaces and the OMCI path as far as the switch driver carries it. The GPON
MAC has a page of its own,
Documentation/networking/device_drivers/pon/rtl9602c-gpon.rst, and so have the
optics, Documentation/networking/device_drivers/pon/rtl9602c-optics.rst.

The path of a frame, from the switch to the fibre::

    host
     |  SerDes lane
    port 0 (sfp)                           port 3 (CPU)
    +-------------------------------+      +-------------------+
    |          switch core          |------| DMA engine (cpu)  |--- CPU
    +-------------------------------+      +-------------------+
    port 2 (pon)
     |  upstream: frames with a stream    downstream: frames with a flow
    +--------------------------------------------------------------+
    | PON MAC                                   rtl9602c-ponmac.c  |
    |   packet buffer: upstream half, downstream half              |
    |   upstream: stream -> queue -> T-CONT                        |
    +--------------------------------------------------------------+
     |  GEM frames
    +--------------------------------------------------------------+
    | GPON MAC                                    rtl9602c-gpon.c  |
    |   GTC framing, PLOAM, O1 to O5, bandwidth map                |
    |   Alloc-ID table, Port-ID table, GEM counters                |
    +--------------------------------------------------------------+
     |  PON SerDes, analog front end
    +--------------------------------------------------------------+
    | optics                                    rtl9602c-optics.c  |
    |   laser driver on I2C master 1, TX_DISABLE, diagnostics      |
    +--------------------------------------------------------------+
     |  fibre
    OLT

Upstream, a frame that the switch sends to port 2 carries a stream, which
names a queue. The queue belongs to a T-CONT, and the T-CONT sends its queues in
the allocations that the OLT grants to its Alloc-ID. For a GEM port the stream
is its flow, the GPON MAC's own number for the port, and the flow gives the
frames their GEM Port-ID. Downstream, the GPON MAC takes a GEM frame whose
Port-ID is in its table into the flow of that table entry, and the downstream
half of the packet buffer hands it to port 2 with the flow. The ONU's OMCI
channel, the OMCC, is the GEM port of flow 64. The CPU sees the OMCC's messages
and the frames of the GEM interfaces through the CPU port's DMA engine, the
conduit of DSA, which has its own page:
Documentation/networking/device_drivers/ethernet/realtek/rtl9602c.rst.

The words of this page:

.. list-table::
   :header-rows: 1
   :widths: 20 80

   * - Term
     - Meaning
   * - GEM port
     - One logical connection on the PON, identified by the 12-bit GEM Port-ID
       in the header of its GEM frames (ITU-T G.984.3 clause 8.3.1).
   * - Alloc-ID
     - The identifier of an upstream bandwidth allocation. The OLT grants
       upstream time to Alloc-IDs. The default Alloc-ID equals the ONU-ID.
   * - T-CONT
     - A transmission container: the traffic that the ONU sends in the
       allocations granted to one Alloc-ID. The scheduler of the PON MAC has
       17 of them, 0 to 16, and the driver refers to a T-CONT by this index.
   * - OMCC
     - The ONU management and control channel, the GEM port that carries OMCI.
       The driver gives it flow 64, T-CONT 16, queue 64 and stream 64.
   * - Flow
     - The GPON MAC's number, 0 to 127, for a GEM port: its entry in the
       Port-ID table and its per-flow configuration. Flow 64 is the OMCC's.
   * - Stream
     - The PON MAC's upstream classification, 0 to 64. A GEM port's stream is
       its flow number, and stream 64 is the OMCC's.
   * - Queue
     - One of the PON MAC's 65 upstream queues, 0 to 64. A stream names a
       queue, and a T-CONT schedules the queues in its map.
   * - Half
     - The upstream or the downstream half of the packet buffer. A register
       written ``X(d)`` in this page has one address for each half, given as
       up / down.

The order of events on the PON side, from the boot of the kernel:

1. Probe. ``rtl9602c_ponmac_probe()`` looks up the system controller and the
   two memory regions of the packet buffer in the device tree. It reads nothing
   in the block. A system controller that has not probed yet defers the probe.
   The code treats a device tree without them as "no PON MAC" and leaves the
   switch working (see "The buffer's DRAM").
2. Setup. The driver creates its debugfs directory and views, registers the
   optics' I2C adapter and hwmon device, creates the OMCI port, and registers
   the GEM data path with the PON class. The last two happen only when the
   system controller, the buffers and the CPU port's conduit exist.
3. ``ip link set pon up``. The first bring-up of the PON builds the PON MAC
   (see "Building the PON MAC"); every bring-up programs the GEM ports again.
   Until the build succeeds, no GEM interface can be created and no OMCI
   message crosses.
4. The OLT activates the ONU. Configure_Port-ID binds the OMCC, which brings
   the OMCI port's link up. Assign_Alloc-ID binds Alloc-IDs to T-CONTs, which
   starts the upstream half of the GEM ports on those T-CONTs.

Device tree and binding
-----------------------

The ``switch`` node of ``arch/mips/boot/dts/realtek/rtl9602c.dtsi`` describes
the switch, and with it the GPON MAC, the PON MAC and the I2C masters of the
optics, which are in its register window. The board file ``dfp-34x-2c3.dts``
adds the two memory regions of the packet buffer. The system controller is a
node of its own. The nodes the PON side uses, without the ports::

    sysctl: syscon@18000000 {
            compatible = "realtek,rtl9602c-sysctl", "syscon";
            reg = <0x18000000 0x1000>;
    };

    switch: switch@1b000000 {
            compatible = "realtek,rtl9602c-switch";
            reg = <0x1b000000 0xf10000>;
            interrupt-parent = <&intc>;
            interrupts = <8>;
            tx-disable-gpios = <&gpio0 13 GPIO_ACTIVE_HIGH>;
            realtek,sysctl = <&sysctl>;

            /* from dfp-34x-2c3.dts */
            memory-region = <&pbo_us>, <&pbo_ds>;
            memory-region-names = "pbo-us", "pbo-ds";

            ports { ... };
    };

and, in ``dfp-34x-2c3.dts``::

    reserved-memory {
            #address-cells = <1>;
            #size-cells = <1>;
            ranges;

            pbo_ds: pbo@16ff000 {
                    reg = <0x016ff000 0x101000>;
                    no-map;
            };

            pbo_us: pbo@1eff000 {
                    reg = <0x01eff000 0x101000>;
                    no-map;
            };
    };

The binding is
``Documentation/devicetree/bindings/net/dsa/realtek,rtl9602c-switch.yaml``. The
PON side reads these properties of the switch node; the others belong to the
switch and to DSA (Documentation/networking/dsa/rtl9602c.rst).

.. list-table::
   :header-rows: 1
   :widths: 30 70

   * - Property
     - What the driver does with it
   * - ``reg``
     - The register window, ``0xf10000`` bytes from ``0x1b000000``. It holds
       the GPON MAC, the PON MAC and the I2C masters as well as the switch
       core.
   * - ``interrupts``
     - One interrupt for all the switch's sources, the GPON MAC's among them.
   * - ``tx-disable-gpios``
     - Optional. The hard transmit-disable input of the laser driver; the
       optics page describes it.
   * - ``realtek,sysctl``
     - A phandle to the system controller, looked up as a syscon regmap. The
       system controller holds the enable in front of the PON MAC. Without it
       the PON MAC is not available.
   * - ``memory-region``, ``memory-region-names``
     - The two DRAM areas of the packet buffer, found by the names ``pbo-us``
       (upstream) and ``pbo-ds`` (downstream). Each must be at least 1 MiB
       (``RTL9602C_PBO_DRAM``) and ``no-map``. The binding requires
       ``realtek,sysctl`` and ``memory-region-names`` with ``memory-region``.
       On this board each is 1 MiB and 4 KiB, of which the driver programs the
       first 1 MiB. Without either one the PON MAC is not available.

The placement of the two regions in DRAM and the rest of the board's device
tree are on the page for the SoC and the board,
Documentation/arch/mips/rtl9602c.rst. The facts about the regions are under
"The buffer's DRAM".

Interfaces to user space
------------------------

- ``ip link set pon up`` starts the ONU's activation and, the first time, builds
  the PON MAC. ``ip link set pon down`` stops the activation and leaves the
  PON MAC built.
- The PON class (Documentation/networking/pon.rst): the OMCI port
  ``/dev/pon0omci0``, the class directory ``/sys/class/pon``, which holds
  ``pon0`` and ``pon0omci0``, and the generic netlink family ``pon``. Both
  ``/dev/pon0omci0`` and the GEM interfaces exist only when the PON MAC is
  available.
- The ``gem`` network interfaces of the PON class, created on ``pon`` by
  management, in practice the OMCI daemon through rtnetlink. In ``ip``
  syntax, for an ``ip`` that knows the kind::

    ip link add gem0 link pon type gem port-id 1000 tcont 0

  ``ethtool -S gem0`` shows ``pon_tx_octets``, the octets the port has sent
  upstream (see "GEM ports as network interfaces").
- debugfs, in the directory ``/sys/kernel/debug/rtl9602c/``, which every block
  of the driver adds its views to. This page describes ``ponmac``, ``ponpath``
  and ``pondp`` (see "The debugfs views"). The GPON MAC creates ``gpon``,
  ``gpontbl``, ``ploamlog``, ``usburst``, ``gemusintr``, ``bwmap`` and
  ``uscntr``, and the optics ``a2`` and ``eeblocks``.
- The hwmon device ``rtl9602c_optics`` (temperature, supply voltage, bias
  current, transmitted and received power of the optics) and the I2C adapter
  ``rtl9602c i2c``, which the optics page describes. The hwmon device exists
  only with ``CONFIG_HWMON`` (on in the board's configuration).
- ``devlink`` on the switch, for the parameters and the region that the
  devlink page describes (Documentation/networking/devlink/rtl9602c.rst); the
  ONU's serial number is set there.
- The kernel log lines that begin ``rtl9602c-switch:``. Those of the PON MAC
  and the GEM ports:

     :header-rows: 1
     :widths: 50 50

     * - Line
       - When

How to read the fact tables
===========================

Each block below has a short account of what the hardware does and what the
driver does with it, followed by fact tables with three columns: *Fact*, *Tag*
and *Checked by*. The tag says how the fact is established. **M**, measured on
the board (the DFP-34X-2C3): *Checked by* gives the command or observation,
through the driver's own interfaces (the kernel log, ``ip``, ``ethtool``,
debugfs), that shows it. **T**, tested by function: the driver depends on the
fact, and the named behaviour fails if it is wrong. **P**, public standard:
*Checked by* names the document and the clause. **I**, inferred, not verified:
the fact is an assumption, and *Checked by* says what would confirm or refute
it. Where the driver writes a value that no measurement or test has shown to
matter, the value is **I**, and the register and field it goes into are **T**
only where a working function needs them. The last chapter collects the **I**
facts and says which of them the driver depends on.

A row with two tags is established in two ways, or in two parts; *Checked by*
says which part carries which tag. Register offsets are in the switch's
register window, physical ``0x1b000000``, unless a row says that they are the
system controller's. The PON MAC starts at ``0xf00000`` in that window. "Up"
and "down" are the two halves of the packet buffer, ``RTL9602C_PBO_US`` (0) and
``RTL9602C_PBO_DS`` (1); a macro written ``X(d)`` has one address for each
half, given as up / down. "Every build" means the kernel log line of the build
(see "Interfaces to user space") in every logged boot of this kernel.

The behaviours that a tested fact depends on are named in words:

- the board boots to a shell with the switch driver probed;
- OMCI messages pass both ways over ``/dev/pon0omci0``: the daemon completes a
  MIB upload and the OLT keeps the ONU in O5;
- GEM data passes both ways through the packet buffer, in throughput runs with
  traffic on the board;
- bridged traffic passes both ways, sourced from the host;
- the downstream locks, and the ONU reaches O5 and holds it on an OLT;
- the *routed-traffic checks* are cases run with traffic on the board; a row
  that cites one says what it shows.

The enable in the system controller
===================================

Every register of the PON MAC sits behind an enable in the system controller,
the block of the SoC that holds its state and the enables of its on-chip
blocks. While the enable is clear, a read anywhere in the PON MAC never
completes: the CPU stalls on the bus and the board stops, with the console, the
network and ssh silent. Nothing before Linux sets it. The switch driver finds
the system controller as a syscon regmap through the ``realtek,sysctl``
phandle of the switch node, sets the enable as the first act of the build of
the PON MAC, reads and writes nothing in the block before that, and never
clears the enable. For the same reason, a tool that reads the switch's window
by address, such as a boot-loader command or ``devmem``, must not read at or
above ``0xf00000`` before a bring-up of the PON has built the PON MAC. The
switch page describes the same enable from the switch's side
(Documentation/networking/dsa/rtl9602c.rst). The offsets in the first rows are
the system controller's.

.. list-table::
   :header-rows: 1
   :widths: 58 6 36

   * - Fact
     - Tag
     - Checked by
   * - Every register of the PON MAC is behind
       ``RTL9602C_SYSCTL_BLOCK_EN_PKTBUF``, bit 5 of
       ``RTL9602C_SYSCTL_BLOCK_EN`` (offset ``0x63c`` of the system
       controller). While the bit is clear a read anywhere in the block never
       completes: the CPU stalls on the bus and the board stops, console,
       network and ssh silent.
     - M
     - Reads of the block with the bit clear stopped the board each time they
       were tried: a single read at the boot-loader prompt, and reads from
       Linux. With the bit set by hand at the boot-loader prompt, a read of
       offset ``0xf0006c`` returned a value.
   * - A stall behind the enable is not reliably recovered by the watchdog: in
       one stall it reset the board after about 36 s, in another it did nothing
       for minutes and the board needed a power cycle.
     - M
     - Two stalls from Linux.
   * - Nothing before Linux opens the enable: at hand-over from the boot loader

The packet buffer
=================

The packet buffer has two halves with the same register set at different
addresses. The upstream half, ``RTL9602C_PBO_US``, carries everything the
switch sends to the PON; the downstream half, ``RTL9602C_PBO_DS``, carries
everything the PON delivers to the switch. Each half keeps frames in pages, in
its on-chip SRAM first and then in its DRAM region (see "The buffer's DRAM").
The driver sets both halves up for 128-byte pages. Between a half and the
switch there is a DMA engine and a MAC that faces the switch, and the build
writes settings into both. The build stops the DMA engines and disables the
halves before it reconfigures them, and enables the halves and then the DMA
engines again before it waits for the halves to finish initialising.

Halves, pages and pools
-----------------------

A half's memory is two pools, one of SRAM pages and one of DRAM pages. The
registers take the page counts written as count minus 1, and the pool limits
as page levels at which a pool counts as exhausted. The driver takes the sizes
of the two SRAMs and of the DRAM pool from its own definitions
(``RTL9602C_PBO_SRAM_US``, ``RTL9602C_PBO_SRAM_DS`` and ``RTL9602C_PBO_DRAM``);
of the region in the device tree it uses the start and checks the size.

each stream the number of its queue, and an admission bit for each stream lets
its frames into the queue; a frame of a stream that is not admitted is not
queued. A queue reaches the fibre through a T-CONT: each T-CONT has a map, a
bitmap over the queues of its group, and an enable bit, and the scheduler sends
the queues in the map of an enabled T-CONT in the allocations that the OLT
grants to the T-CONT's Alloc-ID. Each queue has its own scheduling settings,
which the driver sets to strict priority, committed rate 0, peak rate the
maximum and weight 1, so that a queue sends as much as its T-CONT is granted.

The chain from a frame to the fibre, and the numbers the driver gives it::

    stream s  --(stream map, admission bit)-->  queue q
    queue q   --(bit in the map of T-CONT t)--> T-CONT t, if enabled
    T-CONT t  --(allocations of its Alloc-ID)-> GPON MAC, then the fibre

    GEM port:  stream f --> queue 32 x (t / 8) --> T-CONT t, t = 0 to 15
    OMCC:      stream 64 --> queue 64 --> T-CONT 16
    unused:    streams 0 to 63 --> queue 63, not admitted, in no T-CONT's map

The build points the streams that no GEM port uses, 0 to 63, at queue 63 and
does not admit them. No T-CONT that the driver enables has queue 63 in its map,
so the frames of a parked stream are never scheduled. A stream starts parked,
and a deleted GEM port's stream is parked again. Stream 64, the OMCC's, is
pointed at queue 64, which the build attaches to T-CONT 16.

What a queue drain depends on
-----------------------------

A queue is emptied before it is attached to a T-CONT, so that it is attached
empty. The drain is a command to the PON MAC that the hardware takes at once
and finishes later; the driver polls the busy bit (see "Draining a queue"
under "Sequences"). On a cold boot the drain of the OMCC's queue ends before
the first step of the clock. It does not end while the GPON MAC is still in
the state that a warm reset left it in, which is why the bring-up takes the ONU
to O1 before it builds the PON MAC. The driver times in steps of 10 ns, but its
polls sleep until the next 4 ms tick, so a duration that it reports ends at the
poll that saw the end.

GEM ports as network interfaces
===============================

The switch driver registers the ONU behind its PON port with the PON class,
with 128 flows and 16 T-CONTs, and management creates one ``gem`` interface on
``pon`` for each GEM port (Documentation/networking/pon.rst). A GEM interface
is one flow of the GPON MAC: the flow holds the port's Port-ID in the GPON
MAC's Port-ID table and the type of its downstream traffic, and it is the
port's upstream stream in the PON MAC, which takes the port's frames to a queue
of its T-CONT. The driver programs the downstream half of a port when the port
is created, and the upstream half once the OLT has given the port's T-CONT an
Alloc-ID. That Alloc-ID arrives in a PLOAM message, and the OMCI that creates
the port arrives separately, in either order.

A frame of a GEM interface goes to the conduit directly, with its flow in a
port-mux dst, and a frame that arrives from the PON port in a flow other than
the OMCC's goes to the GEM interface of that flow. In a bridge, the switch
forwards between the GEM ports and the bridge's other ports itself, treating
every GEM port as its one PON port.

The class lists what ``gem_add`` refuses (an upstream port on a T-CONT of 16 or
above, a PON MAC that is not built, a Port-ID already in use, no free flow); the
facts below give what the driver programs.

Bridges and VLANs
-----------------

The switch keeps isolation, learning and VLAN membership for each of its ports,
and it sees every GEM port as its one port 2. The bridge side of the GEM
interfaces follows from that. A frame that the switch forwards from a
subscriber port carries no stream of its own, so classifier entries give it
one; the driver rebuilds them from the GEM side's record whenever a bridge
membership or a VLAN changes (see "Bridging the GEM ports" in
Documentation/networking/dsa/rtl9602c-bridging.rst).

The OMCI channel
================

OMCI messages (ITU-T G.988) cross the PON port in the flow of the OMCC, the
ONU's OMCI channel. Downstream, the GPON MAC takes a message in flow 64 and
the downstream half of the packet buffer passes it to port 2; the switch traps
it to the CPU port and marks it in the receive descriptor, and the conduit
offers it, with its source port, to the OMCI handler of the switch driver
(``rtl9602c_omci_rx()``). The handler removes four bytes that the hardware adds
after every message, and hands the message to the PON class's OMCI port if the
ONU is in O5. Upstream, a message written to ``/dev/pon0omci0`` goes to the
conduit with port 2 and stream 64 (``rtl9602c_omci_tx()``); stream 64 is queue
64 of T-CONT 16, which the allocations of the default Alloc-ID feed. The driver
neither checks nor computes the message integrity check (MIC): the daemon
behind the port builds every message whole. The OMCI port is described on the
PON class page (Documentation/networking/pon.rst), and the conduit's side of
the path on the CPU port page
(Documentation/networking/device_drivers/ethernet/realtek/rtl9602c.rst).

Sequences
=========

The steps are in the order the driver takes them. The tag in parentheses at the
end of a step is the tag of the fact the step rests on (see the tables above);
a step tagged I rests on an assumption.

Building the PON MAC
--------------------

``rtl9602c_ponmac_init()`` is called at the start of every bring-up of the PON
(``ip link set pon up``), after the ONU has been taken to O1 and before the
SerDes is switched to GPON mode.

1. Return at once if the block is already built; return ``-ENODEV`` if the
   system controller or the buffers are missing. A build that fails is logged
   as a warning and the activation goes on: the ONU reaches O5 without OMCI, and
   the next bring-up runs the whole build again. Purpose: the block is built
   once per boot, and the switch works without it. (M)
2. Set ``RTL9602C_SYSCTL_SOC_STATE_READY``, then
   ``RTL9602C_SYSCTL_BLOCK_EN_PKTBUF``. Purpose: open the enable before any
   access to the block. (M for the enable, I for the first bit)
3. Write the token clock. Purpose: the clock of the queue rate limiters; it is
   the first write into the block. (I)
4. Write the committed-rate force words. Purpose: the driver writes them whole.
   (I)
5. Invert the dying-gasp polarity. Purpose: the supply-loss detector. (I)
6. Write the grant thresholds. Purpose: thresholds on the remainder of a grant.
   (I)
7. For T-CONTs 0 to 15, clear the enable bit and empty the map; leave T-CONT 16
   as found. Purpose: no T-CONT schedules a queue that the driver has not
   attached, and the parking queue stays unscheduled. (T)
8. Set the peak-rate drop. Purpose: what exceeds a queue's peak rate is dropped.
   (I)
9. For every queue, 0 to 64, set strict priority, committed rate 0, peak rate
   the maximum and weight 1. Purpose: a queue sends as much as its T-CONT is
   granted. (T)
10. Set the OMCI trap priority to 7. Purpose: the priority of the OMCI frames
    the switch traps to the CPU. (I)
11. Point streams 0 to 63 at queue 63 without admitting them; point stream 64 at
    queue 64 and admit it; name stream 64 as the OMCC's in
    ``RTL9602C_OMCI_OMCC_CFG``. Purpose: a new GEM port's stream starts parked,
    and the OMCC's stream is ready. (M for the streams, I for the naming)
12. Build the packet buffer (next list). Purpose: frames can cross the PON MAC.
    (T)
13. Point the downstream stream 64 at buffer queue 0. Purpose: the OMCC's
    messages take a queue towards the switch. (I)
14. Set the control-frame bit of port 2, then give port 2 its pause marks, then
    set the control-frame bit of port 3. Purpose: the PON control frames pass,
    and the PON port has its own margin. (I)
15. Read the chip revision; on revision 1, turn the peak-rate drop off again.
    Purpose: the exception for the one revision it was written for. (I)
16. Attach queue 64 to T-CONT 16 (see "Attaching a queue to a T-CONT"), then
    point stream 64 at queue 64 once more. Purpose: the OMCC's queue is in a
    T-CONT that the default Alloc-ID's allocations feed. (T)
17. Mark the block built and log "PON MAC built: ...". Purpose: nothing touches
    the block before this point; later bring-ups find it built and never tear it
    down. (M)

Building the packet buffer
--------------------------

``rtl9602c_pbo_init()``, step 12 of the list above.

1. Stop the DMA engine of both halves, then disable both halves. Purpose:
   nothing runs while the buffer is reconfigured. (T)
2. For each half, write the DRAM base, the pool page counts and the pool
   limits. Purpose: the pools, and the region the DRAM pool lives in. (T for the
   base, I for the counts and limits)
3. Write the upstream stop mark and the global and per-stream marks, and the
   downstream off level and then the on level. Purpose: back-pressure and flow
   control. (I)
4. Put both halves in GPON framing; set the upstream receive stop; set the
   upstream FIFO start and space; select 128-byte pages in both pools and both
   engines. Purpose: the framing and the page size the driver assumes. (T for
   the framing, I for the rest)
5. Enable both halves. Purpose: they can initialise. (T)
6. Set the DMA debug selection. Purpose: the value the driver assumes. (I)
7. Set the switch-facing MACs. Purpose: the values the driver assumes. (I)
8. Write the DMA control words whole, which enables the engines last. Purpose:
   the engines run once everything else is set. (T)
9. Wait for ``RTL9602C_PKTBUF_STAT_INIT_DONE`` in the upstream half and then
   the downstream half. Purpose: a queue drain issued earlier does not
   complete. (M)

Attaching a queue to a T-CONT
-----------------------------

``rtl9602c_pon_queue_add()``, for the OMCC's queue in the build and for each
upstream GEM port.

1. Compute the queue number from the T-CONT and the queue index
   (``RTL9602C_PON_QUEUE()``). Purpose: bit q of the T-CONT's map is queue q of
   its group. (M)
2. If the T-CONT's map is empty, set its enable bit. Purpose: the first queue
   switches the T-CONT on. (M)
3. If no T-CONT of the group has the queue in its map, drain it (next list); a
   drain that does not end fails the attach. A queue already in a map carries
   other GEM ports' frames and is not drained. Purpose: a new queue is attached
   empty, and a queue in use loses nothing. (T)
4. Add the queue to the T-CONT's map. Purpose: the T-CONT schedules it. (T)
5. Set the queue strict, committed rate 0, peak rate the maximum, weight 1.
   Purpose: it sends as much as the T-CONT is granted. (T)
6. Admit every stream whose entry already points at the queue. Purpose: frames
   that were waiting for the queue now enter it. (T)

Draining a queue
----------------

``rtl9602c_pon_drain()``.

1. Read the upstream pool in use. Purpose: report it with the outcome; a pool
   with nothing in it and a queue that will not empty are different faults. (T)
2. Write the queue in ``RTL9602C_PONQ_DRAIN_CMD_IDX`` with
   ``RTL9602C_PONQ_DRAIN_CMD_BY_QUEUE`` and ``RTL9602C_PONQ_DRAIN_CMD_START`` in
   one write. Purpose: start the drain. (M)
3. Poll ``RTL9602C_PONQ_DRAIN_CMD_BUSY`` back to back for up to
   ``RTL9602C_PON_DRN_US`` (10,000 us). Purpose: every drain that has succeeded
   needed no more. (M)
4. If it is still busy, poll every 100 us up to ``RTL9602C_PON_DRN_SLOW_US``
   (500,000 us). Ending here is a warning with the time taken and the pool at
   the start; not ending is an error with the register, the pool and whether
   ``RTL9602C_PKTBUF_STAT_INIT_DONE`` was set, and ``-ETIMEDOUT``. Purpose: tell
   a slow queue from one that cannot empty. Both budgets are the driver's; what
   one read of this window costs is not measured. (T)

Programming a GEM port
----------------------

``rtl9602c_gem_program()``, at the creation of a port, for every port after
each bring-up, and for the ports of a T-CONT when an Alloc-ID is bound to it.

1. If the port carries downstream traffic, put its Port-ID in the flow's
   Port-ID table entry. Type the flow's downstream traffic as Ethernet, with the
   multicast bit for a multicast port, whatever the direction. Purpose: the GPON
   MAC takes the port's downstream frames into the flow and hands them on as
   Ethernet. (M)
2. If the port carries upstream traffic and its T-CONT holds no Alloc-ID, log
   "upstream parked" and stop here. Purpose: a T-CONT without an Alloc-ID is
   granted nothing, and its queue would lose the frames at the switch's ingress.
   (P, M)
3. Attach queue 0 of the T-CONT's group (see "Attaching a queue to a T-CONT").
   Purpose: the queue the stream will name is scheduled. (T)
4. Write the port's Port-ID into the flow's upstream configuration. Purpose:
   the GPON MAC puts it in the headers of the port's upstream frames. (M)
5. Point the flow's stream at the queue and admit it. Purpose: the port's frames
   reach its T-CONT. (M)

Deleting a GEM port
-------------------

1. Take the port out of its bridge. Purpose: the switch stops forwarding to it.
   (M)
2. If a routed flow names the port, forget it and rebuild the classifier's
   stream entries without it. Purpose: no entry names a flow that is gone. (T)
3. If the block is built, unadmit the flow's stream and point it at queue 63,
   zero the flow's upstream Port-ID and downstream type, and clear its Port-ID
   table entry. The queue stays in its T-CONT's map. Purpose: the flow is free,
   and its stream is parked. (T)

Known limitations
=================

- At most 64 GEM ports send upstream. The GPON MAC has 128 flows, and the
  driver registers all of them with the PON class, so up to 127 GEM interfaces
  can be created (flow 64 is the OMCC's), but the upstream streams are
  numbered 0 to 64, and stream 64 is the OMCC's. A port that sends upstream
  takes the lowest free flow from 0 to 63, and the 65th such port is refused
  with ``-ENOSPC``; a downstream-only port takes the lowest free flow of all,
  so downstream-only ports use flows that upstream ports could have used
  first.
- The driver attaches only queue 0 of a T-CONT's group. Every GEM port on
  T-CONTs 0 to 7 therefore sends through queue 0 and every port on T-CONTs 8 to
  15 through queue 32, and two T-CONTs of one group share a queue that sits in
  both of their maps. Which T-CONT's grants then drain it has not been
  exercised. Queues are never detached and T-CONTs never disabled
  after the build, so a deleted port's queue stays in its T-CONT's map until
  the next boot. Only T-CONTs 0 and 16 have carried traffic, so the queue
  numbering of T-CONTs 1 to 15 is not measured.
- Every queue is strict priority with committed rate 0 and the maximum peak
  rate, so a T-CONT sends what the OLT grants it and the driver offers no rate
  limit, priority or weighting between GEM ports on the PON side. The unit of
  the rate fields is not known.
- Some losses are not counted. A frame that the switch forwards to the PON port
  without a stream is dropped in the PON MAC while the switch counts it as sent,
  and upstream frames offered above the grant are lost with no counter that the
  driver exposes moving. A stream whose T-CONT has no Alloc-ID would have its
  frames discarded at the switch's ingress port, where they are counted as
  ingress discards.
- A bring-up programs every GEM port again while all T-CONTs are unbound. A
  port that sends upstream keeps its stream's previous queue and admission until
  the OLT assigns the Alloc-ID again. What becomes of upstream frames sent in
  that window has not been measured.
- All GEM interfaces must be in one bridge, and a VLAN can travel up one GEM
  interface only. The entries that tell GEM interfaces apart by VLAN have not
  been measured with two upstream GEM ports; the OLT the board was tested
  against grants one data T-CONT.
- The OMCI messages have the CPU port's control ring to themselves only while
  the switch gives no other frame to the CPU internal priority 7; one that it
  does, through ``dcb apptrust`` and the PCP or DSCP maps or a port's default
  priority, shares the ring with them. The driver removes four bytes from every
  control frame; whether an extended message carries the same four
  has not been observed, and an extended message without them would lose the
  last four bytes of its MIC.
- A GEM flow's upstream octet register clears when read, so a tool that reads
  it by address takes octets out of the port's ``pon_tx_octets``.
- When the PON MAC cannot be built, the bring-up fails with the build's error
  and ``pon`` stays down with the laser off; bringing it up again tries the
  build again.
- A read anywhere in the PON MAC's window before the PON MAC is built stops the
  board, and the watchdog may not recover it. The driver never reads it before
  the build, and a tool that reads the window by address has to keep to the
  same rule.
- Without ``CONFIG_HWMON`` the driver registers no hwmon device, and the
  diagnostics are read through the ``a2`` debugfs view. The driver's Kconfig
  entry depends on ``I2C``, which the optics' adapter needs, and accepts
  ``HWMON`` only built in when the driver is.

Open questions
==============

These are the facts tagged **I** in the tables above, each with what would
settle it. The first list holds the assumptions the driver depends on: the
values it writes and the behaviour it relies on without a measurement, so the
driver is correct on this board only if they hold. The second list holds the
questions to which the code is indifferent: it works whichever way they
resolve.
